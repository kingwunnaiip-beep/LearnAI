3.1

Resultant Forces

Fres = forces in one direction − forces in the opposite direction

A resultant force is the single force with the same effect as all the forces acting together. Same direction → add. Opposite directions → subtract.

Exam habit: decide the direction first. Balanced forces give a resultant force of 0 N; unbalanced forces give a non-zero resultant in the direction of the larger force.

3.2

Mass, weight & gravity

W = m × g

Mass is the amount of matter / inertia and stays constant. Weight is a gravitational force and changes when g changes.

Units: mass in kg, weight in N, g in N/kg. Convert grams to kilograms before substitution.

3.3

Terminal velocity

Fres = W − drag

As speed rises, drag rises. When drag = weight, resultant force = 0, acceleration = 0 and speed becomes constant.

Release → accelerate → first terminal velocity → parachute opens and drag becomes larger than weight → slow down to a new lower terminal velocity.

3.3

Circular motion

resultant force → centre

Constant speed does not mean constant velocity. The direction changes continuously, so velocity changes and the object accelerates toward the centre.

A larger mass or higher speed needs more inward force. A smaller radius (tighter turn) also needs more inward force.

3.4

Force, Mass & Acceleration

F = m × a

Acceleration depends on resultant force and mass. For the same mass, a larger resultant force gives a larger acceleration. For the same force, a larger mass gives a smaller acceleration.

Use SI units: force in N, mass in kg and acceleration in m/s². Rearrange when needed: a = F/m and m = F/a.

3.5

Momentum & impulse

p = mv   •   FΔt = Δp

Momentum is a vector. Impulse equals the change in momentum. Increasing stopping time reduces average force for the same Δp.

For objects that stick together: total momentum before = total momentum after. Rebounds require a sign convention because direction changes.

3.6

Scalars & vectors

vector = magnitude + direction

Scalars have magnitude only. Vectors have magnitude and direction. Perpendicular vectors combine using Pythagoras and trigonometry.

Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time, energy. Vectors: displacement, velocity, force/weight, acceleration, momentum.
